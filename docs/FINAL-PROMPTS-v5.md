## Risk note for the owner: C31 (Vinnitsa) and C32 (Warsaw)

The prompts stay as the owner chose ("AI, full animation"); this note is for the owner to weigh before credits go on them. BarBot, an outside reviewer, rated C31 the highest-risk clip in the film: in its view, model-made breathing on a real victim seconds before his murder, with the gunman shifting his weight, can come across as an uncanny tech demo and make the execution feel trivial, even though no shot is fired. On C32 it warned that walking feet and swinging coats are where video models most often fail (feet that glide, hands that change shape), and that on people being deported any such artefact or changed face would read as disrespectful. Its implied remedy for both is less animation, not more detail. Our existing safeguards are the reject rules (any new expression, eyes moving to the lens, morphing, gliding feet or anything below the pit rim means the take is not used, with no retry), the 4:3 crops, and the short uses (1.6 s and 1.4 s). If the owner wants to lower the risk without dropping the clips, the options are camera-only motion with people held, or a slower camera; both cost the "thrilling video" rule something, which is why this stays the owner's call.
